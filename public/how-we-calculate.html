How we calculate

What the Habitat check does, in plain words, and how sure we are of each part.

Calculated by fixed rules; no AI model runs when you use TankLook; sources were gathered with AI assistance. The same tank gives the same answer every time, and every line the check shows comes from a rule you can read here. The rules and their numbers were collected from published care guides and stocking lists, and an AI model helped gather and sort those sources. We do not claim the rules were reviewed by a panel of experts.

What the check looks at

  • Tank minimums: the smallest tank each animal is kept in, from the sources we found.
  • Social needs: group size, the mix of males and females, pairs.
  • Environment: temperature, water chemistry, cover, lid, and the gear the animals need: a heater and a filter.
  • Behavior: who eats, nips or fights whom.
  • Spatial fullness: how much of the room is used, below.

The verdict word (Compatible, Caution, Problem) is the worst of the checks that ran. A check that could not run, because an input is missing, is shown as not checked and never turns the verdict grey. A heater or filter the animals need but the tank does not have is a Caution with a Fix it button; it is never shown as green.

Room used

One number, labelled for what it is: how much of the tank's swimming room (or floor, for bottom dwellers) the animals use. It is not the verdict and it does not include filtration, temperature or behavior, which are checked separately.

Comfortable (under 75%)Full · Over past 100%
  1. Each animal takes room by its body size: bigger and more active animals need more. Body size comes from published length and weight data.
  2. Schooling fish share room. Up to 10 animals each count in full. Past 10, every extra animal adds less: n animals count as 10 × (n ÷ 10)0.72.
  3. Taller water gives swimmers more room, with diminishing returns. Bottom dwellers are judged by floor area instead.
  4. The total is divided by the room the tank has. 75% is where experienced keepers tend to stop, 100% is the limit.

The constants were fitted to 31 AqAdvisor answers (our number divided by theirs has a middle value of 1.01), then animals under 2 g were eased to 80% of that room so that 37 published expert stocking lists read about 74% at their stated maximum. That is a judgment, and it is tagged as one below.

Worked example: 10 cardinal tetras in a 40 gallon breeder

Tank36 × 18 × 16 in, 44.9 gal
10 cardinal tetrasRoom used 27%, Comfortable. AqAdvisor reads 29% for the same tank.
40 cardinal tetrasRoom used 73%: the 40 count as 27 for room, so they read about 2.7 times the 10, not 4 times.

On common cases we land close to AqAdvisor. We do not claim to be looser or stricter than other calculators; they use different data and on some tanks they read higher or lower than we do. What we add is that we show the working: what is counted, how much it counts as, and how many more of the same animal fit before 75% and 100%.

The same 10 cardinals with no heater are a Caution, not green: cardinals want 73–84 °F and a room-temperature tank would sit near 70 °F. Room used says nothing about that; the Environment check does.

A rule that depends on something we cannot see, such as the sex of your animals or your room temperature, says what it depends on, and stays a Caution until you state it.

What it does not do

  • It does not measure your water. Temperatures are expected from the heater and the room you enter (70 °F is assumed if you do not), never measured.
  • It does not count feeding, water-change habits or how long the tank has run, except where a line says so.
  • It is a guide, not a promise. On the 31 AqAdvisor tanks we compared against, our room number was more than 10% above theirs on 8, more than 10% below on 11, and within 10% on 12. Treat Caution as a reason to read the line, and ask a keeper you trust.
