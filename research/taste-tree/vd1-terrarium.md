# VD1 terrarium archetypes (six picker families, land side)

Researched 2026-10-30. US units. Prior context: C-marine-brackish-land.md nodes (land-*). Sources are hobby / retailer care sheets unless noted; numbers vary by source and are given as typical ranges. Confidence tags: [ok] = several sources agree, [weak] = thin or conflicting.

## 0. Decisions in one table

| Family | Final archetypes | Change from draft |
|---|---|---|
| Tropical Rainforest | dart-frog-rainforest, rainforest-canopy | rainforest-canopy re-split: glass, dense, planted, crested gecko. Chameleon mesh cage moves out to branch-canopy only |
| Desert & Drylands | rocky-desert-canyon, basking-scrubland, dune-flats (was sandy-dunes) | sandy-dunes kept but renamed and made packed-mix by default (safety flag S1). Droppable if the team wants fewer tiles |
| Enchanted Woodland | mossy-woodland, fairy-forest | Both plant-led, no vertebrate default (flag S2). fairy-forest is the weakest; fallback is a "whimsy props" toggle on mossy-woodland |
| Rocks, Cliffs & Canopies | cliff-wall-climber, branch-canopy | branch-canopy = open screen chameleon tower only (no crested gecko) |
| Botanical Worlds | closed-jar-garden, bioactive-planted, succulent-garden | succulent-garden is valid and animal-free; keep open-top and dry |
| Creature Showcases | snake-hideaway, tarantula-burrow, jumping-spider-loft, tortoise-table | tortoise-table minimum raised to 6x3 ft (flag S4) |

Merges and look-alike calls:
- rainforest-canopy (Tropical Rainforest) vs branch-canopy (Rocks, Cliffs & Canopies): look-alikes if both are "tall, branches, gecko or lizard". RE-SPLIT by container and light, not animal: rainforest-canopy = sealed-looking glass box, wet foliage wall, cork back panel, soft misty light; branch-canopy = open black screen mesh on all sides, a bare branch lattice, bright lamp up top, wide air gaps. If testers still confuse them, merge into one "Tall Canopy Tank" and move the glass / mesh choice to the welfare layer.
- dart-frog-rainforest vs rainforest-canopy: both green tall glass. Split by where the eye goes: dart = floor-level (leaf litter, pool, bromeliads, tiny frog on a leaf, no empty vertical space); canopy = vertical lanes (branches, hanging vines, open air column, gecko clinging high).
- bioactive-planted vs dart-frog-rainforest: bioactive-planted is the same plants with no vertebrate; split by a visible leaf-litter/cork "forest floor" with white-and-blue isopods and a low box. If it reads as "dart frog minus frogs", fold into dart-frog-rainforest as an "animal-free" toggle.
- mossy-woodland vs fairy-forest: same greens; split by props (toadstools, tiny arch, stepping stones) and light (warm glow). Weak split.
- rocky-desert-canyon vs basking-scrubland vs dune-flats: horizontal stacked slate and boulders (canyon), flat open ground with one basking pile and scrub (scrubland), smooth sculpted sand waves with almost no stone (dunes). Distinct if each keeps its signature surface.
- cliff-wall-climber vs rocky-desert-canyon: cliff = TALL stacked-ledge back wall, little floor; canyon = LONG low boulders and floor. Different shape (tall vs long).
- Hermit crab omitted (draft had none); prior note "isopod / hermit crab low distinctness" is handled by bioactive-planted.

## 1. Safety flags (all must ship as rules, not tips)

- S1 sandy-dunes: loose sand is contested. Sources split: one camp says any loose substrate risks impaction in leopard geckos and similar; another says husbandry (heat) is the root cause. No vet-level source found. Agreement: pure calcium sand is worst; packed topsoil/sand/clay mixes (about 60/30/10 or 40/40/20) are the common safe choice; wild leopard geckos favour packed clay. Rule: default substrate for every desert tile is a packed sand/soil/clay mix rendered sand-coloured. Pure loose play sand only unlocks for sand-adapted specialists (sand boa, dune gecko, sandfish), 3-4 in deep [weak source]. Never calcium sand. Never default a dune look for leopard gecko or bearded dragon.
- S2 Enchanted Woodland animals: fire salamander and other caudates carry the Bsal risk; USFWS listed 201 salamander species as injurious (2016) barring import and interstate transport, and wild-caught natives are an ethics / legality problem. Fire-bellied toads are half water (1-2 in water over about half the floor) so they belong in a paludarium, not a land woodland. No amphibian default. Safe guests: captive-bred isopods, springtails, American giant millipede. Plant-led is the honest answer.
- S3 Gradients: every heated tile keeps a warm and a cool end and at least two hides (warm and cool). Density "open" never removes hides, cover, climbing or the gradient; it removes decor only.
- S4 tortoise-table: the draft 4x2 ft is below every adult guideline (sources: 6x3 ft, 22 sq ft, even 8x8 ft). Use 6x3 ft minimum; a 4x2 ft table is hatchling-only.
- S5 Cohabitation: single animal default for crested gecko (males fight), chameleon (strictly solo), tarantula, jumping spider, snake, tortoise. Dart frogs: same species groups only, never mixed species. No frog + gecko, no mixed-reptile defaults.
- S6 Chameleon is a high-care animal (mist or drip schedule, UVB, live plants, no glass). Do not label easy. Tagline carries the catch.
- S7 Closed jar: overwatering and direct sun are the failure modes; no animals ever. Do not add isopods to a sealed jar.
- S8 Rock hazards: stacked rock must be glued/secured (cliff wall, canyon); loose stacks crush animals.

## 2. Tropical Rainforest

### dart-frog-rainforest "Jewel Frog Jungle"
- Tagline: "Tiny jewels in a living jungle. Needs daily mist."
- Fingerprint: front-opening tall glass cube; cork-bark or foam back panel with moss; bromeliads, pothos, creeping fig; leaf litter floor with a shallow pool; one or three bright blue / yellow frogs on a leaf; mist droplets on glass.
- Contrast: vs rainforest-canopy, everything is floor-level and packed; no tall open air column.
- Scene: 18x18x18 to 18x18x24 front-opening glass; cork left, central log with bromeliads, 2 vines, moss hill, leaf litter, small water dish, shallow pool.
- Density: open = clear leaf-litter floor, 2 hides, 3 plants; balanced = 6 plants, background moss; full = canopy fill with retained hides and a visible open patch for the frogs.
- Render needs: cork back panel, moss, leaf litter, bromeliad rosettes, water droplets, water surface, small frog models with colour variants.
- Temp 72-78 F day (up to 80), never above 82-85. Humidity 80-100%, misted several times daily or automated.
- Min enclosure: 18x18x18 in (about 25 gal; 20 gal high for 1-3 frogs) [ok].
- Animals: Dendrobates / Ranitomeya group of one species (blue, yellow, green), cleanup crew of springtails and isopods.
- Welfare: same-species group only; drainage layer; good ventilation to avoid stagnant wet air; leaf-litter hiding places that stay in every density; no wild-caught.
- Src: https://joshsfrogs.com/lp/dart-frogs----individually-pictured--ipad ; https://azeah.com/node/383 ; https://bantam.earth/vivariums/

### rainforest-canopy "Vine Canopy Tank"
- Tagline: "A tall green climb for a gecko. Needs lots of height."
- Fingerprint: tall glass box (taller than wide); cork flats and hanging vines on the back; high branches with a hide; live pothos, philodendron; crested gecko clinging to the glass or a vine; open air column in the middle.
- Contrast: vs dart-frog-rainforest, a vertical open lane and branches instead of dense floor; vs branch-canopy, glass and wet foliage instead of open mesh and a lamp.
- Scene: 18x18x24 (or 18x18x36 better), coir floor, 3 branches, 2 high hides, cork back, pothos.
- Density: open = 3 branches + 1 plant; balanced = 5 branches + vines + 3 plants; full = foliage wall with open flight lane and both hides.
- Render: cork flats, vines, sphagnum floor, branches at heights, leaf cover, simple gecko model.
- Temp 72-78 F (at or below 80), night 65-72 F. Humidity about 60-80% with a dry-out between mists (40-80% cycling also reported) [ok].
- Min: 18x18x24 in (about 34 gal) adult [ok]; 18x18x36 preferred.
- Animals: one crested or gargoyle gecko; optional captive-bred tree frog variant not default.
- Welfare: one adult, no cohabitation; no heat lamp above 80 F; climbing and hides never reduced; vertical cork not slippery plastic.
- Src: https://www.cbreptile.com/?p=122163 ; https://www.pangeareptile.com/blogs/blog/basic-care-for-crested-geckos ; https://reptilesmagazine.com/?p=931

## 3. Desert & Drylands

### rocky-desert-canyon "Rocky Desert Canyon"
- Tagline: "Warm red stone and a gecko. Keep the cool end."
- Fingerprint: long low box; stacked slate / red sandstone ledges; a boulder hide; one aloe or haworthia; warm tan-orange tones; a gecko on flat rock; a small moist hide.
- Contrast: vs cliff-wall-climber, horizontal and floor-led; vs basking-scrubland, tall rock features and shade, not open flat ground.
- Scene: 36x18x16 (40 gal breeder), sand/topsoil/clay mix, two rock hides (warm, cool), 1-2 slate shelves, one dry plant, bulb or heat source on one end.
- Density: open = 2 hides + 1 shelf; balanced = 3 stones + shelf + plant; full = canyon of stacked slate with 3 hides and clear walkway.
- Render: slate / sandstone slabs, boulder hide, tan clay-sand surface, heat-lamp glow.
- Temp: warm surface 88-92 F, cool end 72-77 F, night no lower than about 65 F. Humidity 30-40% with a moist hide at 60-80%.
- Min: 20 gal long for one leopard gecko; 40 gal breeder (36x18x16) as the render baseline.
- Animals: leopard gecko, fat-tailed gecko.
- Welfare: packed mix not loose sand (S1); moist hide; secured rock (S8); no cohabitation of males.
- Src: https://reptifiles.com/leopard-gecko-care/leopard-gecko-substrate/ ; https://www.zenhabitats.com/blogs/reptile-care-sheets-resources/leopard-gecko-complete-substrate-guide ; https://bantam.earth/vivariums/

### basking-scrubland "Basking Scrubland"
- Tagline: "Wide-open sun for a big lizard. Needs real UVB."
- Fingerprint: large 4 ft box, bright lamp, a tall slate basking pile at one end, dry branches, sparse hardy scrub; flat open ground; spiky lizard on the stone.
- Contrast: vs rocky-desert-canyon, big open sun-lit floor and one basking pile rather than tall shaded canyon.
- Scene: 48x24x24, sand/soil mix 4-6 in, slate slab basking pile, branch, hide, dry plants outside the animal's reach.
- Density: open = basking pile + 1 hide; balanced = + branch + rocks + 1 plant; full = adds extra hides and décor but keeps open basking run.
- Render: lamp glow, slate pile, dry branches, scrub plants, sand-soil ground.
- Temp: basking surface 100-110 F, hot side 85-90, cool side 75-80, night 72-78 F. Humidity 30-40%. UVB across half the length.
- Min: 4x2x2 ft (about 120 gal) [ok].
- Animals: bearded dragon (single adult).
- Welfare: UVB tube replacement (about 6 months); no loose sand for a sick or small dragon; one animal; hides never removed.
- Src: https://www.phoenixherp.com/assistance/reptile-care-guides/bearded-dragon-care-guide.html ; https://healthtopics.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/node/1181 ; https://www.zenhabitats.com/blogs/reptile-care-sheets-resources/bearded-dragon-essential-care-information

### dune-flats "Dune Flats" (replaces sandy-dunes; conditional)
- Tagline: "Rolling sand, a hidden burrower. Keep it dry and sparse."
- Fingerprint: smooth sand ridges and ripples; almost no stone; one stone arch or half-log; a single hardy tuft; the animal usually buried, only a head or tail showing.
- Contrast: vs rocky-desert-canyon, sculpted sand with no stone mass; vs basking-scrubland, low and secretive, no basking pile.
- Scene: 36x12x12 or 20x10x12 box; 3-4 in sand-colored substrate, shaped dune; half-log hide; flat warm stone.
- Density: open = dune + 1 hide; balanced = + 2nd hide + flat stone; full = adds a small rock arch with open sand running.
- Render: sand dune mesh with ripple normal, depth cutaway, fine grain.
- Temp: warm 85-90 F, cool 75-80 F [weak]. Humidity 20-40%.
- Min: 20 gal long (sand boa) [weak]; 10 gal for dune gecko (20x10x12).
- Animals: Arabian or Kenyan sand boa, dune gecko, sandfish skink.
- Welfare: S1: renders as packed sand/clay mix unless a sand specialist is chosen; never calcium sand; keep at least 3-4 in depth; hides and gradient intact even in "open".
- Src: https://www.furrycritter.com/pages/products_gear/snakes/arabian_sand_boa_housing.htm ; https://thetyedyediguana.com/care-sheets/dune-gecko-stenodactylus-sp ; https://reptifiles.com/leopard-gecko-care/leopard-gecko-substrate/

## 4. Enchanted Woodland (thin; plant-led)

Temperate / montane terrarium looks that exist: moss-and-fern mossariums, forest-floor terrariums with leaf litter and rotted log, cloud-forest ferneries (60-72 F), fairy-garden bowls, and bioactive cool terraria with isopods. Animal fits are narrow: no amphibian default (S2); invertebrates only.

### mossy-woodland "Mossy Woodland"
- Tagline: "Soft moss, ferns and a fallen log. Stays cool."
- Fingerprint: thick moss carpet, a diagonal rotting log, ferns, scattered leaf litter, moody low light, no brightly coloured animals; a wisp of mist.
- Contrast: vs fairy-forest, no props, deeper green and brown, dark and still; vs bioactive-planted, temperate plants and log not tropical pothos and cork.
- Scene: 18x18x18 to 24x12x18 glass, moss hill left, log diagonal, 2 ferns, leaf litter, small pool optional, springtails.
- Density: open = moss carpet + log; balanced = + ferns + stones; full = ferns and moss close but keeps a bare patch.
- Render: moss layers (cushion, sheet), rotted log with bracket fungus, fern fronds, leaf litter, soft fog.
- Temp 60-75 F (no heat lamp), humidity 70-90%, cooler is better.
- Min: 18x18x18 in (about 25 gal).
- Animals: none required; optional captive-bred American giant millipede, isopods, springtails.
- Welfare: no heat; ventilation to prevent mold; no wild-collected moss or animals; no salamander default (S2).
- Src: https://www.furrycritter.com/pages/products_gear/reptiles/slimy_salamander_housing.htm ; https://joshsfrogs.com/care-sheet/american-giant-millipede-narceus-americanus-care-guide ; https://terrariumtribe.com/bioactive-terrarium/

### fairy-forest "Fairy Forest"
- Tagline: "A tiny toadstool glade with glow. Plants only."
- Fingerprint: red-capped toadstools, a tiny arch or stepping-stone path, bright mossy lawn, a warm glow light, maybe a miniature door.
- Contrast: vs mossy-woodland, props and brightness; plants and moss are the same by design.
- Scene: 12x12x12 cube or a wide shallow bowl under a cloche; 5 mushrooms (large, small), moss, 2 fittonia or ferns, pebble path.
- Density: open = lawn + 3 toadstools; balanced = + path + plants; full = toadstool cluster and plants, path stays visible.
- Render: stylised mushroom caps and gills, emissive warm glow, small props, pebble path.
- Temp 65-75 F; humidity 70-90%.
- Min: 12x12x12 (about 7.5 gal).
- Animals: none (springtails invisible).
- Welfare: plants only; do not add animals; mushrooms are decor or substrate-grown fungi, not edible; keep away from pets.
- Src: https://terrariumtribe.com/bioactive-terrarium/ ; https://www.gcshop-sg.com/blogs/knowledge-hub-start-an-isopod-bioactive-terrarium/what-is-a-bioactive-terrarium

## 5. Rocks, Cliffs & Canopies

### cliff-wall-climber "Cliff Wall Ledges"
- Tagline: "A stone cliff with crevice hides. Warm top, cool bottom."
- Fingerprint: tall stacked-ledge back wall (slate, ledgestone or textured foam), small floor, crevices and cork tubes, trailing plant on a ledge, a gecko on the wall.
- Contrast: vs rocky-desert-canyon, vertical wall dominates and floor is minimal.
- Scene: 18x18x24 tall glass; full-height rock back wall with 3-4 ledges, crevice hides, cork tube, 1 hanging plant, sand-soil floor.
- Density: open = wall + 2 ledges; balanced = 4 ledges + plants; full = wall layered with ledges, trailing plants and 3 crevice hides, free mid-air.
- Render: layered rock background panel with crevices, cork tube, ledges.
- Temp: warm basking 85-90 F, ambient 78-85, night 70-75; humidity 40-60% (dry-ish; too humid causes respiratory problems).
- Min: 18x18x24 (about 34 gal) for one; 15 gal tall for a Moorish gecko [weak].
- Animals: Moorish / Mediterranean wall gecko, rock gecko. No tokay default (aggressive biter), no chuckwalla default (needs a large cage).
- Welfare: glue wall to stop rockfall (S8); gradient and hides retained at every density; captive-bred only.
- Src: https://vetverified.com/articles/moorish-gecko-the-tenacious-climber-of-the-mediterranean ; https://thetyedyediguana.com/content/care-sheet-pdfs/Crocodile-Gecko-Tarentola-mauritanica-care-sheet.pdf

### branch-canopy "Chameleon Branch Tower"
- Tagline: "An open leafy tower for one chameleon. Mist twice daily."
- Fingerprint: tall black screen mesh cage (no solid glass), a branch lattice, ficus or hibiscus leaves, lamp on top, mister or drip nozzle, one chameleon on a branch.
- Contrast: vs rainforest-canopy, open mesh, lamp, bare branch lattice, no cork wall or misty glass.
- Scene: 24x24x48 mesh enclosure; 3 horizontal and 2 diagonal branches, ficus, pothos, dripper, basking light at top.
- Density: open = 3 branches + 1 plant; balanced = 5 branches + 3 plants; full = foliage cover with open basking branch and drinking spot.
- Render: mesh texture, branch lattice, leaves, lamp glow, drip droplets.
- Temp: basking 85 F (up to 95-100 F in some guides), ambient 72-78, night 55-65. Humidity 40-60% (mist peaks higher). UVB.
- Min: 2x2x4 ft (about 120 gal) for one adult [ok].
- Animals: veiled chameleon (hardy choice).
- Welfare: solo only; not glass; daily misting or drip; live non-toxic plants; hides are foliage, kept at every density; high-care label.
- Src: https://reptifiles.com/veiled-chameleon-care/ ; https://www.petmd.com/reptile/veiled-chameleon-care-sheet ; https://www.thebiodude.com/blogs/chameleon-caresheets/veiled-chameleon-caresheet

## 6. Botanical Worlds

### closed-jar-garden "Closed Jar Garden"
- Tagline: "A sealed mini-world. Water once, bright indirect light."
- Fingerprint: lidded glass jar or bottle, condensation on the glass, tiny moss and fittonia, pebbles at the base, no animal.
- Contrast: vs succulent-garden, sealed and wet-green; vs bioactive-planted, plants only, no leaf litter or critters.
- Scene: 1-5 gal jar, gravel and charcoal base, soil, moss hill, 2-3 small plants, tiny stone.
- Density: open = moss and 1 plant; balanced = 3 plants; full = packed but with clear glass.
- Render: curved glass, condensation, moss, small leaves.
- Temp: room 65-75 F; humidity 80-100% inside; no direct sun.
- Min: 1 gal jar.
- Animals: none.
- Welfare: not applicable; no animals; overwatering and direct sun failures (S7).
- Src: https://tropicalglass.co.uk/blogs/blog/the-science-behind-closed-terrariums-understanding-how-they-work ; https://extension.msstate.edu/publications/how-design-closed-system-terrarium

### bioactive-planted "Forest-Floor Bioactive"
- Tagline: "A planted box with a working bug crew. Keep it damp."
- Fingerprint: low glass or tub, leaf litter and cork flats, moss, pothos or fittonia, visible white-and-blue isopods, springtail speckles.
- Contrast: vs dart-frog-rainforest, no vertebrate and a low litter focus; vs mossy-woodland, tropical plants and cork, crew in view.
- Scene: 10-20 gal glass, cork flats, leaf litter, moss, 3 plants, 12 isopods.
- Density: open = litter + cork + 1 plant; balanced = + 3 plants; full = fills but keeps litter and cork visible.
- Render: leaf litter, cork flats, isopod models, springtails as dots.
- Temp 68-78 F; humidity 70-90%.
- Min: 10 gal.
- Animals: dairy cow or powder blue isopods, springtails.
- Welfare: moisture gradient (dry corner) for isopods; no vertebrates by default; escape-proof lid; never release isopods outdoors.
- Src: https://www.gcshop-sg.com/blogs/knowledge-hub-start-an-isopod-bioactive-terrarium/what-is-a-bioactive-terrarium ; https://terrariumtribe.com/bioactive-terrarium/

### succulent-garden "Succulent Garden"
- Tagline: "Open-top rosettes in a glass bowl. Water sparingly."
- Fingerprint: wide shallow open bowl or tray, rosette succulents, pebble or sand top-dressing, a small stone, bright light.
- Contrast: vs closed-jar-garden, open top, dry and spiky; vs the Desert family, small and animal-free.
- Scene: 5-10 gal bowl; fast-draining cactus mix, pebble top-dress, 3 rosettes, 1 stone.
- Density: open = 2 plants; balanced = 4 plants + stone; full = tight rosettes with a visible pebble surface.
- Render: succulent rosettes, pebble layer, bowl glass.
- Temp: room 65-80 F; humidity room (20-40%).
- Min: 2 gal bowl.
- Animals: none (isopods do not suit dry mixes).
- Welfare: not a vivarium; not sealed; watering only when dry (avoid sealed lid, rot); no animals.
- Src: https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/mg356 ; https://extension.msstate.edu/publications/how-design-closed-system-terrarium ; https://gardenersworld.com/house-plants/how-to-make-a-terrarium

## 7. Creature Showcases

### snake-hideaway "Snake Hideaway"
- Tagline: "A calm coil in a snug hide. Needs heat and a cool end."
- Fingerprint: long low cage; two hides (warm and cool); water bowl; cork branch; fake vines; a coiled patterned snake half in a hide.
- Contrast: vs tarantula-burrow, long low shelves not deep substrate.
- Scene: 48x24x24; cork hides, bowl, branch, 3 vines, heat lamp one end.
- Density: open = 2 hides + bowl; balanced = + branch + vines; full = more cover, both hides and bowl kept.
- Temp: warm hide 86-92 F, cool 72-80 F; humidity 50-60% (45-75 reported).
- Min: 4x2x2 ft (about 120 gal) adult [ok].
- Animals: ball python (single).
- Welfare: both hides; bowl large enough; heat on a thermostat; cohabitation never.
- Src: https://reptifiles.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Ball-Python-Care-Sheet-PDF-1.pdf ; https://www.phoenixherp.com/assistance/reptile-care-guides/ball-python-care-guide.html

### tarantula-burrow "Tarantula Burrow"
- Tagline: "A deep burrow with a hairy tenant. Mostly hides."
- Fingerprint: deep substrate cut-away showing a burrow, half-round cork bark, water dish, sparse plants, the spider at the mouth.
- Contrast: vs jumping-spider-loft, deep ground vs tall bright.
- Scene: 12x12x12; 6 in coir; cork; dish; Chilean rose tarantula.
- Density: open = cork + dish + 6 in substrate; balanced = + plant; full = more cork and plants but the burrow stays.
- Temp 70-80 F; humidity species-specific (50-70% typical).
- Min: floor about 3x leg span; 10 gal for burrowers [weak].
- Animals: terrestrial tarantula (Chilean rose); not for handling.
- Welfare: single; substrate depth at least leg span; hide always; escape-proof.
- Src: https://www.thebiodude.com/blogs/opinion-and-conservation-iniatives/how-big-should-your-tarantula-enclosure-be ; https://www.thetarantulacollective.com/tarantula-thoughts/how-big-should-a-tarantula-enclosure-be

### jumping-spider-loft "Jumping Spider Loft"
- Tagline: "A bright mini-loft for one curious spider. Needs airflow."
- Fingerprint: small cube, twig, leaf, silk nest, tiny colourful spider, mesh vent.
- Contrast: vs tarantula-burrow, tall and bright rather than deep and dim.
- Scene: 8x8x12 to 10x10x12 acrylic cube; cork twig, 2 leaves, silk nest, cross vents.
- Density: open = twig + nest; balanced = + leaf + plant; full = more twigs, clear room to jump.
- Temp 68-80 F (reports 68-76 or 75-85); humidity 50-70% [weak].
- Min: 8x8x12 in (about 3.3 gal) adult small; 10x10x12 preferred.
- Animals: regal jumping spider (single).
- Welfare: strong cross-ventilation to stop mold; one spider; no live prey left.
- Src: https://thetarantulacollective.com/care-sheets-2/phidippus-regius ; https://www.thebiodude.com/blogs/arachnid-caresheets/regal-jumping-spider-caresheet-and-bioactive-habitat-maintenance

### tortoise-table "Tortoise Table"
- Tagline: "A wide grazing table and a warm lamp. Lives for decades."
- Fingerprint: wide, shallow open-top table; sloped soil; slate; weeds and dandelions; humid hide; lamp; a domed tortoise grazing.
- Contrast: vs basking-scrubland, open-top table with green weeds and a tortoise, not a tall lamp-over-rock lizard setup.
- Scene: 72x36 in table; 4-6 in substrate; slate dish; hide; edible weeds.
- Density: open = hide + dish + slate; balanced = + weeds; full = plants and logs while the basking and humid hide stay clear.
- Temp: basking 95-100 F, ambient 75-85, night up to 72 F. Humidity dry, with a humid hide (sphagnum).
- Min: 6x3 ft (S4).
- Animals: Hermann's or Russian tortoise.
- Welfare: 10-12% UVB; dry floor; humid hide; no Sulcata default (huge).
- Src: https://reptilesmagazine.com/Hermanns-Tortoise/ ; https://www.lllreptile.com/articles/48-hermanns-tortoise ; https://www.reptilecentre.com/info-hermanns-tortoise-care-sheet
