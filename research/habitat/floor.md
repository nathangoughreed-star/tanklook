## 2. Formal guidelines
- German BMEL Gutachten 1998 (Mindestanforderungen Zierfische Sueßwasser). Page: https://www.bmleh.de/DE/themen/tiere/tierschutz/haltung-zierfische-tabelle.html . General minimum 54 L, example 60 x 30 x 30 cm (1800 cm2 = 279 sq in), exceptions for small species/breeding. Species table gives VOLUME only (C. aeneus/paleatus/trilineatus 60 L, Pangio 60 L, Ancistrus 80 L, Otocinclus 60 L, Botia macracanthus 150 L, Callichthys 120 L). No separate floor-area rule found; text says only "ausreichend Raum". Gutachten under revision (https://www.bmleh.de/DE/themen/tiere/tierschutz/haltungsgutachten-ueberarbeitung.html), draft has general + species part; I could not read the draft. Implied: 60 L tank footprint is 279 sq in for a group of ~6 corys = 46 sq in each (est).
- Swiss TSchV Anhang 2: tables read via secondary summaries only (Stiftung Tier im Recht, https://www.tierimrecht.org/documents/4599/2023-03-10-fischwissen-ch-Verkauf-tierschutzwidriger-Aquarien-auf-eBay.pdf): aquaria "geraeumig", visual cover required; BLV guidance min 54 L. No floor-area figure found. fedlex page needs JavaScript; unverified.
- Austrian 2. Tierhaltungsverordnung Anlage (54 L, 60x30x30) seen via summaries; PDF unreadable. Unverified whether it has length-based formulas.
- UK/RSPCA, OATA, zebrafish/lab guides: nothing floor-based found. Zebrafish guidance is per litre (5/L, up to 12/L; Univ. Mississippi IACUC https://tss.research.olemiss.edu/sites/default/files/Guideline%20for%20Zebrafish%20Stocking%20Density.pdf ); no area figures.

## 3. Surface area rule
"1 inch of fish for every 12 square inches of surface area" (20 for wide-bodied) https://fishtanksdirect.com/blog/how-much-swimming-space-does-your-fish-need/ . Basis is oxygen/gas exchange at the water surface; the page never mentions bottom dwellers or floor. In a straight-sided tank surface area equals floor area, so numerically it is a floor figure: 12 sq in per inch means k = 12/L (L=3 in gives k=4; L=2 gives 6; L=16 gives 0.75). No source applies it to bottom dwellers (our inference). Note it is linear in length, not quadratic, so it gives bigger k for small fish and small k for big fish.

## 4. Density studies (benthic, per m2)
No Corydoras/loach study found. Aquaculture analogues, not transferable to ornamental welfare:
- Channel catfish fingerlings, 10/15/20/25 fish/m2 (16 m2 tanks): lowest density gave largest fish, highest worst FCR (https://www.ucc.ie/en/media/research/planforbio/pdfs/IrwinEtAl1999Aqua.pdf is a hit but I did not confirm it is this study). 10-25/m2 = 400-1000 cm2 = 62-155 sq in per fingerling (size unknown).
- Marine catfish Plotosus lineatus: growth ok to 50/m2, 25/m2 judged best (=400 cm2 = 62 sq in each).
- African catfish Clarias: density effect depended on size; crowded fish clustered with low aggression (https://edepot.wur.nl/122091).
- Winter flounder: no growth/aggression difference across range (https://scholars.unh.edu/jel/361).
These come from search summaries; I did not open the papers.

## 5. Snails
No source on floor/glass area. Only count rules: nerite "one per 5 gallons" or 2-5 gal per snail (https://modestfish.com/?p=4062, https://www.aquariadise.com/nerite-snails/); mystery snail 2-4 per 10 gal. Nothing on swimming room (snails crawl; need surface/glass/decor, our inference). Floor area is not the constraint in any source.

| Plotosus 25/m2 | 62 sq in | unknown | n/a |

Reading: small bottom schoolers (cory, kuhli) land at k about 3-7, centre about 5; k=4 is at the low-to-middle end. Large loaches (clown) imply k about 1 (their rule is group-driven and quadratic scaling over-predicts). Suggest floor area = max(k x L^2 for small, ~12 x L linear cap) or k decreasing with size (est).
