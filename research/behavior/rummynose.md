# Rummynose tetra (Hemigrammus rhodostomus / bleheri): ethogram (R208)

Source: a ChatGPT answer to the compact-plus prompt, gathered by Nathan on 2026-11-28 (cites experimental schooling studies on this species). This is a digest; the full answer is in the C25 chat transcript.

## Character
"The school is an emergent behavior, not a rigid formation." Each fish reacts to its neighbors' position, heading and movement while accelerating, coasting, avoiding obstacles and chasing food on its own. Danger tightens the group, comfort loosens it, feeding breaks it up for a while, darkness dissolves it (they school by sight).

## Movement mechanics
Starts with tail beats that build up, into smooth swimming with a steady head. Brakes and steadies with the pectorals. Turns by bending and sweeping the tail. Hovers level with small fin corrections. Startle: a sharp whole-body bend, then hard tail beats, almost instant acceleration. **Cruising is really burst-and-coast**: each fish accelerates, glides and slows, with its timing independent of its neighbors (shown in experiments), so fish keep swapping positions.

## Ethogram
**Moving:** cruises in step with the group (very often, middle and lower-middle water); burst-and-coast (very often); the whole school reverses, with the turn passing from fish to fish (often); follows the glass, then turns away (often); fish swap places inside the school (very often); curves around plants and rock (often); the school splits around an obstacle and joins up again (sometimes); swims into the current (sometimes).
**Resting and daily rhythm:** hovers level (often); loose shoal when relaxed, headings no longer matched (often); hovers quietly near cover (sometimes); spreads out in the evening as the light dims (sometimes, keeper-reported); rests hovering at night, nearly still (normal); the red head fades at night (often); brightens and regroups in the morning (normal).
**Feeding:** anticipation, gathering at the feeding spot when the keeper moves (often); the formation breaks up to chase particles (often); intercepts drifting food (often); takes food at the surface or from the bottom (sometimes); chases live food in short bursts (often); races others to a particle without real aggression (sometimes); regroups after feeding (often).
**Social:** lines up with neighbors (very often); corrects spacing, veering off when too close and closing in when too far (very often); a lost fish speeds up to rejoin the group (often); in a small group they're skittish, stay near cover and school less consistently (often); a large group is confident and can relax into a loose shoal (a bigger school is NOT necessarily tighter); occasionally chases its own kind; ignores peaceful fish (very often); steers away from big fish (sometimes); briefly travels with similar tetras (rare).
**Startle and stress:** one fish's startle burst spreads through the school into a group escape (sometimes); bunches up while moving away (sometimes); scatters in all directions in a severe scare (rare); regroups gradually afterwards; the nose fades under stress (transport, bullying, illness, bad water), which is different from the normal fading at night; stress listlessness, clamped fins; erratic swimming or jumping (rare); gasping near the surface (rare).
**Breeding:** egg scatterer, rarely observed: male chases female near fine plants, eggs scattered, adults eat eggs. No parental care. Detailed courtship would be speculative.

## What a generic animation gets wrong (its list)
Constant speed instead of burst-and-coast; tail beats in sync; fixed positions or a permanent leader; every fish turning at once instead of the turn passing along; a permanently tight school regardless of danger; random wandering as the daytime default; schooling in the dark; resting on the bottom; a nose that is always fully red; territory, aggression or a cave.
