# Kribensis (Pelvicachromis pulcher): ethogram (R208)

Source: a ChatGPT answer to the compact ethogram prompt, gathered by Nathan on 2026-11-28. This is a digest; the full answer is in the C25 chat transcript.

## Character
A bonded pair that lives in a home territory (a cave plus the bottom around it). Purposeful moves with pauses, not aimless cruising. Aggression is triggered by fish coming close, not random, and they break off the chase and return. Breeding changes everything: movement, aggression, cave use, territory size.

## Ethogram
**Everyday**
- Cruises locally (often): smooth and deliberate with frequent turns, low between plants and hardscape, short bouts that end in a pause.
- Hovers in place (often): level body, gentle pectoral strokes, just above the substrate or near cover.
- Forages in the substrate (often): head down, picks and spits out sand.
- Intercepts food (at feeding): a burst toward sinking food, grabs it, slows.
- Inspects caves (sometimes): peers in, enters, comes out.
- Rests (regularly): hovers quietly under wood or plants or inside a cave. Lying belly-down on the bottom is NOT documented, so it differs from corys and gobies.

**Social and territory**
- Pair stays loosely together (often): same home area, not synchronized.
- Holds a territory (habitual): keeps returning to and patrolling a cave plus the bottom around it. That's usually one section or one end of the tank, and in a small tank during breeding it can be most of the bottom.
- Threat display (sometimes): faces the intruder, fins up, stiff posture, sometimes turns side on.
- Territorial chase (sometimes; often when breeding): a sudden charge, then a sharp turn back. Mostly aimed at other bottom fish; tetras passing overhead are ignored.
- Extended pursuit (occasional): chases across the tank when the intruder persists or breeding aggression is high. No fixed distance is documented.
- Border standoffs (occasional): flaring and short charges with neighbors, especially other kribs or cichlids.
- Physical fights (uncommon in a compatible community).

**Startle and stress**
- Escape dart into shelter (sometimes). Hides until things calm down.
- A subordinate avoids the bully: gets pinned in a corner or near the surface and leaves its normal territory.
- Colour changes with stress or agitation (patterns depend on context).

**Breeding and parental care**
- Female courtship: arches her body, shows her red belly, quivers toward the male, near the chosen cave.
- Male answers: spreads his fins, follows her toward the shelter.
- Digging (often before spawning): both carry sand away in their mouths to clear the cave entrance and dig pits.
- Female stays in the cave for long stretches, and the male stays close by.
- Spawning: eggs on the inside surfaces of the cave.
- Egg care, mostly the female: fans the eggs and picks at them.
- Guards the perimeter, mostly the male (almost constantly): hovers at the entrance, patrols outward, intercepts fish.
- Territory grows during brood care, and they tolerate fewer tankmates.
- Herds the fry: moves slowly with a cloud of fry below and behind.
- Signals to the brood: body twitches, fin flicks and colour changes call the fry to gather or settle.
- Picks up stray fry in the mouth and spits them back into the group. Moves the brood to another shelter.
- Defends together: one parent stays with the fry while the other drives off the threat.
- Rejects its partner, or drives off older young (sometimes).

## What a generic animation gets wrong (its list)
Roaming the whole tank instead of keeping a home territory; schooling; resting on the bottom like a cory; caves not used as destinations; random rather than proximity-driven aggression; endless chases instead of breaking off and returning; identical male and female roles; a territory that doesn't grow when breeding starts or the fry move; leaving out digging, quivering, brood signals and carrying fry in the mouth.
