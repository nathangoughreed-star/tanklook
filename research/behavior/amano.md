# Amano shrimp (Caridina multidentata): ethogram (R208)

Source: a ChatGPT answer to the scoped prompt, gathered by Nathan on 2026-11-28. This is a digest; the full answer is in the C25 chat transcript.

**Character:** a surface-oriented, opportunistic forager that is "almost always fiddling with something". **Plants, wood, rock and glass are one connected terrain at any angle**, and swimming mostly links one surface to the next. **The antennae and feeding claws keep up nearly continuous secondary motion.** Social contact only happens when shrimp converge on food: no bonds, no territory.

**Movement:** walks on alternating thin legs, body steady, antennae sweeping ahead. Climbs by gripping uneven spots: sideways, up, upside down. Swims with rhythmic swimmerets, body stretched out, walking legs tucked loosely. When still it grips the surface while its mouthparts and antennae keep moving. **Escape: a sudden abdomen curl and tail fan launches it backward**, then it rights itself and swims off or grabs a surface. The tail is never used for normal forward swimming.

**Ethogram:** walks while grazing (very often, nearly anywhere); walks briskly to food or cover (often); climbs stems and leaf edges (often); **walks upside down under leaves and overhangs** (often); walks on glass (sometimes); swims forward (sometimes, brief); hops between surfaces (often); rests stuck to a surface (sometimes); shelters in plants or under hardscape (sometimes); forages at night (often). **Picks fast with alternating claws** (very often); tugs and gathers algae strands (often); picks over individual substrate grains (often); grazes wood, rock and moss (often); heads for food that goes in (often); **swarms food from all sides** (often); **grabs a piece and carries it somewhere quieter** (often); holds a big piece under its head and picks at it (often); takes food at the surface, flipped over (occasionally reported); scavenges dead tissue. Loose clusters on rich surfaces; antennae touch on meeting; steps around others; big shrimp shove in at food; tug-of-war over a piece; steals fish food; moves away from fish. Grooms its legs and antennae (often); freezes when startled; tail-flick escape, sometimes several in a row; frantic swimming (stress, or males searching for a female, which looks similar); climbs out of the water (occasionally); stress stillness. Molt: withdraws beforehand, sheds (rarely seen), hides afterwards, the empty shell gets eaten. Females fan carried eggs.

**Generic-animation mistakes (its list):** swimming too much instead of walking and climbing; body kept horizontal on every surface; claws still while grazing; symmetrical or looping antennae; tail flicks used for normal swimming; coordinated groups; food fights as long combat; never hanging under leaves; fresh molts out in the open; treating the floor as the main habitat instead of every surface.
