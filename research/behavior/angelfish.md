# Freshwater angelfish (Pterophyllum scalare): ethogram (R208)

Source: a ChatGPT answer to the scoped prompt, gathered by Nathan on 2026-11-28. This is a digest; the full answer is in the C25 chat transcript.

**Character:** a hovering, visually alert, hierarchical cichlid organized around favourite spots. The default is calm hovering or a deliberate move, interrupted by feeding, inspecting, displays and sudden bursts. Each fish keeps **lasting relationships** (rank, familiarity, a partner) and favourite resting places. Pairing reorganizes where everyone sits.

**Movement:** gentle moves on pectorals plus small tail strokes, stronger beats to accelerate. Brakes and hovers with fine fin adjustments, **swims backward** without turning. Tight turns staying upright, spinning on its vertical axis with little rolling or banking. The tall dorsal and anal fins make small stabilizing moves, and **the pelvic filaments trail and bend** with movement and flow. Slips **edge-on between vertical stems**, but its height blocks low openings. Bursts are far faster than its cruising.

**Scape use:** hovers among tall stems (very often), blending in; weaves through stems upright (often); hangs under overhanging wood without touching it (often); crosses open water purposefully (often); turns on the spot (very often); rises and sinks staying upright (often); inspects or pecks leaves and wood (sometimes); picks at the bottom (sometimes). It does NOT burrow, sift, perch on the bottom or live in caves.

**Social and displays:** loose group (often), casual following; **rank displacement**: a dominant fish approaches and the lower one moves aside without contact (often); a dominant fish holds the best spot (often); lateral threat with fins spread and mouth open; frontal display, head up; open-jaw threat; body-undulation display; **lip-locking** (a contest, not affection); dominance chase; nip; subordinate retreat, backing off then turning; submission posture with fins in, head up, darker colour. Pairs: a partner preference forms, the pair swims together, inspects a leaf or wood face together, and defends that site and lunges at other species that come close. Prey drive: stalks and strikes at small fish and shrimp.

**Startle and stress:** freezes; bursts toward plants; hides; stripes and colour change, fins clamped.

**Generic-animation mistakes (its list):** constant forward swimming; exaggerated body wave at slow speed; decorative pectorals; rolling or banking on turns; rigid or paddling pelvic filaments; ignoring vertical plants; tetra-style schooling; equal access for every fish; random aggression instead of rank, territory and history; lip-locking shown as kissing; bursts barely faster than cruising.
