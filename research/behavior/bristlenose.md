# Bristlenose pleco (Ancistrus sp.): ethogram (R208)

Source: a ChatGPT answer to the compact-plus prompt (movement mechanics, 25-40 entries), gathered by Nathan on 2026-11-28. This is a digest; the full answer is in the C25 chat transcript.

## Character
"A surface-bound grazer with intermittent swimming ability, not a swimming fish that occasionally lands." Its life is finding a surface, attaching, grazing, resting, and retreating to a familiar shelter. **The surface sets its orientation, not gravity**: belly to the surface on glass, slopes and upside down under overhangs. Hidden by day and adventurous after lights-out (established fish also feed by day). A mature male's cave and brood override almost everything else.

## Movement mechanics
Crawls more than it swims: it repositions its suction mouth, makes small fin movements and flexes the body and tail, advancing in small steps. To turn, it moves the head and swings the rear around. Braces its fins while holding on with the mouth, and can breathe and rasp while attached. For longer trips it lets go completely, swims with strong tail strokes and quickly reattaches. When startled it bolts explosively, with no wind-up. Can back out of caves.

## Ethogram
**Moving:** glides on glass in small steps (often); crawls along wood, bending around the contours (very often); crawls over rock (often); scuttles low over sand (often, brief); swims freely (sometimes, brief, near the bottom); hops from one surface to another (often); pivots while attached (often); backs out of crevices (sometimes); crawls upside down under wood or cave ceilings (sometimes).
**Resting:** in a cave (very often, long); stuck to glass, wood or rock (often); flat on the bottom (often); hanging upside down (sometimes); wedged into a crevice (sometimes); at the cave mouth, half exposed (often); holding station facing into the flow (sometimes).
**Feeding:** rasps algae (very often); rasps along the wood grain (very often; a surface grazer, not a true wood-eater); grazes leaves (sometimes); damages soft plant leaves (occasional, with poor diet); forages on the bottom (often); sits on a wafer (often, long); clamps onto a vegetable (often, long); leaves shelter when food goes in (often).
**Social:** ignores most fish (very often); grazes near other plecos without coordinating (sometimes); shoves others off food (sometimes); threat display, fins spread with cheek spines out (sometimes, mostly at rival plecos); short territorial chase and return (sometimes); males fight over caves, shoving, thrashing and locking cheek spines (occasional); rasps the slime coat of large slow fish (rare, abnormal).
**Startle and stress:** bolts to cover (occasional); clamps down tight instead of fleeing (sometimes); hides even from food (stress); colour lightens or mottles (sometimes); frantic swimming (rare, abnormal); trips to the surface for air (occasionally reported, varies by Ancistrus species).
**Breeding:** males claim a cave (often in mature males); male clears the inside of the cave; female inspects the cave while the male displays at the entrance; the male keeps the female inside during spawning; orange egg cluster on the cave ceiling (rarely seen); **the male fans (very often) and guards almost constantly, eating less**; removes bad eggs (occasionally reported); fry leave the cave, cling to surfaces and start grazing as guarding fades.

## What a generic animation gets wrong (its list)
Constant swimming or hovering; staying upright whatever the surface angle; tail wagging while attached; smooth gliding instead of step-by-step crawling with pauses; moving without visible mouth contact and rasping; following other plecos as a group; the same activity day and night; caves treated as interchangeable rather than owned; gradual acceleration instead of an explosive bolt; animating the bristles like sensing barbels; never staying still for long.
