# Kuhli loach (Pangio kuhlii / semicincta): ethogram (R208)

Source: a ChatGPT answer to the compact-plus prompt, gathered by Nathan on 2026-11-28. This is a digest; the full answer is in the C25 chat transcript. Note: most "kuhlis" sold are P. semicincta or close relatives. This describes the familiar banded aquarium kuhli.

## Character
Concealment, body contact, exploring the substrate, and activity in bursts. "Its world is a network of tunnels, crevices, roots, soft sediment and communal resting places, rather than an open swimming volume." Fish can vanish completely, and the reappearance has to feel natural. An empty-looking tank, a knot of resting loaches, methodical evening foraging and a sudden tank-wide frenzy are all normal.

## Movement mechanics
Snake-like: waves of bending travel down the whole body, not tail-driven. Starts with a head move and a body flex, then slithers. Stops by easing the undulation and settling against whatever it's on, often still curved. Very tight U-turns. Burrows by pushing the head into soft sand and wriggling through until it's gone. Startle: a vigorous whole-body burst **into the nearest cover** (not away into open water).

## Ethogram
**Resting and hiding:** rests hidden under wood, rock or dense plants (very often); resting pile, bodies overlapping and draped (often); head or tail sticking out of cover (often); fully buried in the sand (sometimes, varies by tank); resting out in the open (sometimes); draped over a leaf or root (sometimes); lying partly on its side, limp-looking but normal (occasional); wedged in narrow gaps, often with others (often).
**Moving and exploring:** slithers along the bottom (very often when active); stop-and-go exploring (often); threads through plant roots (often); tunnels under leaf litter (often when present); burrows into sand (sometimes); moves under or within the sand surface (sometimes, poorly documented); climbs along hardscape (sometimes); serpentine swim through open water (sometimes); **the loach dance or frenzy** (occasional): vigorous loops, reversals and vertical runs round the tank, individually or most of the group, often after a water change, sometimes with weather changes or for no reason, fading gradually, not stress; glass surfing (sometimes); jumping (rare).
**Feeding:** probes surfaces with its barbels (very often when active); sifts sand (often); digs head-down with the rear exposed (often); feeds in crevices (often); comes out when food goes in (often, several at once); feeds as a writhing group (often); catches small worms and larvae (sometimes); forages after lights-out (often).
**Social:** explores loosely in a group (often); follows (sometimes); tangles together into a living knot (often); slides over and under companions with no reaction (often); others come out when some become active (sometimes); feeds alongside corys and shrimp (often); yields to bigger fish (sometimes); territorial aggression (very rarely reported).
**Startle and stress:** bolts (sometimes); slips into the nearest crevice, roots or sand (sometimes); stays hidden afterwards (often); persistent frantic swimming (uncommon, stress); hides too much (bad conditions, small groups, bright light, bullying).
**Breeding (low confidence, rarely seen):** courtship chase; a pair rising together; an embrace with bodies intertwined near the surface; green eggs scattered among floating roots. No parental care.

## What a generic animation gets wrong (its list)
Always visible; a rigid body; hovering at rest instead of lying against surfaces; avoiding contact with companions; ordinary schooling; treating the substrate as solid; constant activity; reading the dance as fear; always upright (it rests curved, tilted, sideways, draped); fleeing into open water instead of into cover.
