# Panda cory (Corydoras / Hoplisoma panda): ethogram (R208)

Source: a ChatGPT answer to the ethogram prompt, gathered by Nathan on 2026-11-28. This is a digest; the full answer is in the C25 chat transcript. Qualitative only.

## Character
Social bottom fish that switch between busy foraging, short scoots and complete stillness. Individuals stay independent inside a cohesive group: some rest while others forage, with group bursts of activity that start without any outside trigger. Not a school, not a cave-holder, not territorial.

## Ethogram (frequency word, then a short description)
**Resting and moving**
- Rests on the substrate (very common): upright, belly down, pectorals out, tail truly still. Gills and mouth still move.
- Rests beneath cover (very common): under wood or leaves, sometimes with the head sticking out.
- Rests on a leaf (occasional).
- Scoots along the bottom (very common): short tail pulses, then a pause.
- Swims across the tank (common): smooth, low, then slows and settles.
- Pivots in place (very common): head stays put while the tail swings round.
- Hovers above the bottom (occasional, brief): a transition or an inspection.
- Settles onto the bottom (very common): slows, sinks, pectorals out, tail stops.
- Goes from stillness straight into a fast move, with no wind-up.

**Feeding**
- Searches the sand with barbels (very common): slightly nose-down, shuffling forward.
- Burrows its snout into sand (common): steeper head-down, mouth hidden, small sand puffs.
- Spits out sifted sand through the mouth or gills (common).
- Investigates an object (common): wood, rock, plant bases.
- Searches excitedly when food goes in (whole group): faster, lots of pivots, scattering.
- Feeds from a food item (common): head down on a pellet, crowding in and facing it.
- Catches sinking food (occasional). Doesn't feed at the surface.

**Social**
- Follows another panda (common), travels as a loose group (common), forages alongside others (very common).
- Rests in a cluster (common): touching or overlapping, facing every which way.
- Brushes against others with no reaction, and pushes into a feeding crowd (common). Contact is normal, not a collision.
- Nudges a companion (reported in corys generally; uncertain for pandas).
- Yields to bigger or bossier fish (occasional), wanders back into a cichlid's territory and gets chased out again.

**Distinctive**
- Dashes to the surface for air (intermittent): steep fast climb, a gulp, a sharp turn, straight back down, then carries on with what it was doing. Long gaps between dashes, and sometimes several fish go up close together (shown in bronze corys). It's a brief event, not a fixed breathing cycle.
- Swims up and down the glass (occasional, often in groups, often in corners). Not necessarily stress.
- Swims against the current (occasional): faces the filter flow, drifts back, repeats.
- Explores higher surfaces (occasional): climbs along plants or wood.

**Startle and stress**
- Freezes when disturbed (occasional), darts away (occasional), retreats into cover (occasional, sometimes as a group, sometimes scattering).
- Abnormal: repeated distressed trips to the surface, rubbing on the substrate, persistent hiding. Stress shows as more frequent or more intense normal behaviors, not as new behaviors.

**Breeding** (episodic)
- Gets more active, males chase females, barbel-contact courtship, the T-position (also mid-water, rolling), the female carries eggs between her pelvic fins, searches for a spot, sticks them to glass or moss. Several males compete for one female. No pair bond and no guarding. Eats eggs opportunistically.

## Added from the compact-prompt rerun (same day)
- Explores more in the evening (often): roams and forages more as the light dims.
- No leader: group moves start when one fish leaves and the nearby fish follow one after another, while some stay behind. Group moves come out of each fish reacting to its neighbors.
- A startle dart can set off the neighbors scattering.

## Its own suggestion for the sim (note, not a decision)
Give each fish its own drives (activity, hunger, wish to be near others, need to rest, need for air), and let nearby companions influence when it switches behavior. Some behaviors override everything else: an air dash, the feeding response, escape, breeding. "A rigid schooling algorithm combined with a bottom-height constraint would miss much of what makes panda corydoras recognizable."

## Compared with the peacock gudgeon (first patterns)
- **Shared:** rests on the substrate, settles, stop-go movement, pivots, investigates objects, retreats to cover or darts when startled, feeding frenzy, true stillness at rest, more confident with more cover.
- **Different:** home-site fidelity (gudgeon male's cave vs. no home); group shape (gudgeons keep spacing vs. corys pile up touching); feeding (selective picker vs. sand sifter); cory-only air dashes, glass and current swimming; gudgeon-only lateral displays and egg guarding.
- **Structure both answers point to:** a library of shared behaviors, chosen per species, switched by drives plus triggers, with nearby fish influencing the switches.
