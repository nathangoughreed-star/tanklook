Create a scientifically accurate orthographic reference sheet of a single adult lawnmower blenny (Salarias fasciatus) for 3D model reconstruction.

PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

This is a technical reconstruction reference, not a collection of separate fish illustrations.

Every view must represent the exact same rigid three-dimensional specimen, with identical body proportions, fin attachment positions, anatomical landmarks, and coloration.

Priority order:

1. Accurate species anatomy and body proportions.
2. Exact silhouette consistency between matching views.
3. Correct fin geometry and attachment boundaries.
4. Consistent anatomical landmarks and coloration.
5. Surface detail and illustration quality.

If any requirements conflict, prioritize geometry over visual appearance.
CANVAS AND LAYOUT

* Portrait canvas, 2:3 width-to-height ratio.
* Pure black (#000000) background.
* Five horizontal rows separated by at least 3% of the canvas height of empty black space.
* Row 4 contains two front views side by side, separated by black space.
* All views use exactly the same physical scale.
* No perspective distortion, labels, rulers, borders, shadows, or additional objects.
* Keep all fins and body parts within their assigned view areas.
* Center the views consistently. Side and top views must share the same horizontal snout and caudal-peduncle coordinates.

MASTER GEOMETRY — MOST IMPORTANT
First establish one anatomically accurate, finless three-dimensional body. All five rows must be derived from this same body.

Species proportions:

* Adult lawnmower blenny, standard length 4.5 inches.
* Maximum body depth = approximately 27% of standard length.
* Head depth = approximately 16% of standard length.
* Minimum caudal-peduncle depth = approximately 11% of standard length.
* Adult. Blenny body, elongate and slightly compressed, with a straight back and belly and a blunt, steep forehead.
* The head is blunt, with the profile dropping nearly vertical from the forehead to the mouth.
* Greatest body depth just behind the head.
* Caudal peduncle tapers evenly to the caudal fin.
* Eyes large, high on the top corners of the head.
* Mouth wide, at the front of the blunt snout, closed, with comb-like teeth hidden behind the lips.
* A small, branched tentacle above each eye.
* Snout blunt and gently rounded in the side and top views, never a pointed cone or beak.
* Seen from the front, the closed mouth is a fine lip line on a solid head surface; the nostrils are faint marks on the skin, never dark holes or openings.
* Natural asymmetry of the head and body profile; do not substitute a perfect ellipse or pointed leaf shape.

Important: The front and top views must reflect this slightly compressed anatomy. Do not draw a round disc and do not make the body deep.
MASTER COLORATION
Natural colouring, slightly subdued, not saturated.

The body is olive to brown, with several dark vertical bars, dark streaks toward the front, and many round or elongated white spots of varying sizes. A few small, bright blue spots with dark outlines sit on the upper rear half of the body.

Eye: gold with a dark pupil.

Color boundaries must wrap continuously over the three-dimensional body, remaining consistent in all projections.
FIN GEOMETRY
Attach the fins to the established master body without modifying its shape.

* Dorsal: One long, continuous fin from the nape to the caudal peduncle, low and even, joined to the caudal base by a membrane. Olive-brown with fine dark lines. Drawn opaque.
* Anal: A long, low fin along the rear belly. Olive-brown. Drawn opaque.
* Caudal: Rounded to straight, with a pale edge. Olive-brown with fine dark lines. Drawn opaque.
* Pectoral: Emerging behind the gill cover, rounded, naturally folded close against the flank. Clear with faint marks. Drawn opaque.
* Pelvic: Small, thread-like, below the throat. Pale olive. Drawn opaque.
* No adipose fin.

Use natural fin-ray structure and smooth, anatomically accurate edges. Fins are opaque and not translucent.
