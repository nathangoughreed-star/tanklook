Create a scientifically accurate orthographic reference sheet of a single adult flame angelfish (Centropyge loricula) for 3D model reconstruction.

PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

This is a technical reconstruction reference, not a collection of separate fish illustrations.

Every view must represent the exact same rigid three-dimensional specimen, with identical body proportions, fin attachment positions, anatomical landmarks, and coloration.

Priority order:

1. Accurate species anatomy and body proportions.
2. Exact silhouette consistency between matching views.
3. Correct fin geometry and attachment boundaries.
4. Consistent anatomical landmarks and coloration.
5. Surface detail and illustration quality.

If any requirements conflict, prioritize geometry over visual appearance.
CANVAS AND LAYOUT

* Portrait canvas, 2:3 width-to-height ratio.
* Pure black (#000000) background.
* Five horizontal rows separated by at least 3% of the canvas height of empty black space.
* Row 4 contains two front views side by side, separated by black space.
* All views use exactly the same physical scale.
* No perspective distortion, labels, rulers, borders, shadows, or additional objects.
* Keep all fins and body parts within their assigned view areas.
* Center the views consistently. Side and top views must share the same horizontal snout and caudal-peduncle coordinates.

MASTER GEOMETRY — MOST IMPORTANT
First establish one anatomically accurate, finless three-dimensional body. All five rows must be derived from this same body.

Species proportions:

* Adult flame angelfish, standard length 4.7 inches.
* Maximum body depth = approximately 55% of standard length.
* Head depth = approximately 36% of standard length.
* Minimum caudal-peduncle depth = approximately 12% of standard length.
* Adult dwarf angelfish. Oval, strongly laterally compressed body, deep through the middle, with an evenly arched back and an evenly rounded belly.
* Short head with a steep, gently convex forehead. Blunt and gently rounded snout in the side and top views, never a pointed cone or beak. Small terminal mouth.
* Seen from the front, the closed mouth is a fine lip line on a solid head surface; the nostrils are faint marks on the skin, never dark holes or openings.
* A single short spine points backward from the lower corner of the cheek (preopercle), the same colour as the head, lying flat against the body.
* Greatest body depth at about the middle of the body. Short caudal peduncle.
* Eye round, high on the head, above the mouth.
* Natural asymmetry of the head and body profile; do not substitute a perfect ellipse or pointed leaf shape.

Important: The front and top views must reflect this strongly laterally compressed anatomy. Do not draw a round disc or a pointed snout. Do not draw long trailing fin filaments.
MASTER COLORATION
Natural colouring, slightly subdued, not neon-saturated.

Adult. The head and body are a deep red-orange, more yellow-orange in the middle of the flank and on the lower body. Five narrow, slightly irregular black vertical bars cross the upper and middle flank behind the head, longest in the middle of the body and shortening toward the tail; they do not reach the belly. A short black dash sits on the upper rear of the head behind the eye.

Eye: dark, with a thin orange ring.

Color boundaries must wrap continuously over the three-dimensional body, remaining consistent in all projections.
FIN GEOMETRY
Attach the fins to the established master body without modifying its shape.

* Dorsal: One continuous fin from above the gill cover to the caudal peduncle: a low spiny front part and a taller soft rear part with a rounded, slightly pointed rear corner, no deep notch between them. Red-orange; the rear soft part has alternating black and electric-blue bands along its edge. Drawn opaque.
* Anal: Starts under the middle of the body, a few spines in front and a rounded, slightly pointed soft rear part mirroring the dorsal. Red-orange with alternating black and electric-blue bands along the rear edge. Drawn opaque.
* Caudal: Rounded fan. Yellow-orange, a little paler than the body. Drawn opaque.
* Pectoral: Rounded, emerging behind the gill cover, naturally folded close against the flank. Pale orange. Drawn opaque.
* Pelvic: Pointed, below the pectoral base. Red-orange. Drawn opaque.
* No adipose fin.

Use natural fin-ray structure and smooth, anatomically accurate edges. Fins are opaque and not translucent.

Record each fin's precise attachment boundary to the master body. These attachment boundaries must remain unchanged in every panel.
