Create a scientifically accurate orthographic reference sheet of a single adult green spotted puffer (Dichotomyctere nigroviridis) for 3D model reconstruction.

PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

This is a technical reconstruction reference, not a collection of separate fish illustrations.

Every view must represent the exact same rigid three-dimensional specimen, with identical body proportions, fin attachment positions, anatomical landmarks, and coloration.

Priority order:

1. Accurate species anatomy and body proportions.
2. Exact silhouette consistency between matching views.
3. Correct fin geometry and attachment boundaries.
4. Consistent anatomical landmarks and coloration.
5. Surface detail and illustration quality.

If any requirements conflict, prioritize geometry over visual appearance.
CANVAS AND LAYOUT

* Portrait canvas, 2:3 width-to-height ratio.
* Pure black (#000000) background.
* Five horizontal rows separated by at least 3% of the canvas height of empty black space.
* Row 4 contains two front views side by side, separated by black space.
* All views use exactly the same physical scale.
* No perspective distortion, labels, rulers, borders, shadows, or additional objects.
* Keep all fins and body parts within their assigned view areas.
* Center the views consistently. Side and top views must share the same horizontal snout and caudal-peduncle coordinates.

MASTER GEOMETRY — MOST IMPORTANT
First establish one anatomically accurate, finless three-dimensional body. All five rows must be derived from this same body.

Species proportions:

* Adult green spotted puffer, standard length 5.5 inches.
* Maximum body depth = approximately 38% of standard length.
* Head depth = approximately 30% of standard length.
* Minimum caudal-peduncle depth = approximately 16% of standard length.
* Adult pufferfish, at rest (not inflated). Round-headed, tapering body: thick through the head and chest, tapering behind the dorsal and anal fins to a narrow caudal peduncle.
* Head with a rounded forehead and a short, rounded snout; terminal beak-like mouth.
* Seen from the front, the closed mouth is a fine lip line on a solid head surface; the nostrils are faint marks on the skin, never dark holes or openings.
* Greatest body depth just behind the eyes. Narrow caudal peduncle.
* Eye round, set high on the head, bulging slightly.
* Natural asymmetry of the head and body profile; do not substitute a perfect ellipse or pointed leaf shape.

Important: The front and top views must reflect this rounded, almost as wide as it is deep anatomy. Do not draw an inflated, ball-shaped fish. Do not draw spines.
MASTER COLORATION
Natural colouring, slightly subdued, not neon-saturated.

Adult. Back and upper flanks yellow-green densely covered with round black spots of different sizes. The belly is pure white, with a sharp boundary to the green along the lower flank.

Eye: gold with a dark pupil.

Color boundaries must wrap continuously over the three-dimensional body, remaining consistent in all projections.
FIN GEOMETRY
Attach the fins to the established master body without modifying its shape.

* Dorsal: A single small rounded fin far back on the back, just in front of the caudal peduncle. Clear yellowish. Drawn translucent.
* Anal: A single small rounded fin under the dorsal. Clear. Drawn translucent.
* Caudal: Rounded fan. Yellow-green with dark spots. Drawn semi-translucent.
* Pectoral: Rounded fan behind the gill opening, naturally folded close against the flank. Clear. Drawn translucent.
* Pelvic: No pelvic fins.

Use natural fin-ray structure and smooth, anatomically accurate edges. Fins are opaque and not translucent.
