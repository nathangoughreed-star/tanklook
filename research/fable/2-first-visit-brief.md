## The question (restated at the end)

"Walk through these screens as a brand-new hobbyist and, separately, as an expert with many tanks. Where does each get lost, bored, or misled? Give the 5 changes that would matter most, ranked, each with the screen it affects and what 'fixed' looks like - judged against the product vision."

Only the judgment is wanted. Below is evidence: what I clicked, the visible text, and observations of confusing, slow or broken behaviour (an observation like "button X was below the fold" is a fact about the screen, not a verdict).

## Product vision (Nathan, 2026-11-13, as recorded)

Users are anyone interested in aquariums, every experience level; the UX should make a brand-new hobbyist and someone with 20 tanks both feel at home. Purpose: plan, evaluate, be inspired, learn; design something that fits their needs, preferences and constraints; explore ideas they would not have had; find the best tank for them. Experience level is an OPTIONAL gate, not automatic guidance (Constraints has "Count my experience: optional", off by default). Money matters: an accurate budget, deals, cheaper equivalents. NOT in scope: care guides (cycling, upkeep). The product should "provide the info and experience and customizability that would be hard to replicate in an AI chatbot". No users yet. Welfare is a non-negotiable gate in the stocking engine (HANDOFF "UNIFIED SETUP VISION": never a toggle, never traded for price).

## Timing and instrumentation (measured, fresh profile, software GL)

- Cold load to the Start sheet visible: 5.8 s desktop (4.3 s phone). No console errors or warnings on any screen (zero entries captured across all runs).
- Fastest path to a finished tank: Start sheet -> "Example tanks" -> "Planted community" = 2 clicks; tank and fish on screen about 12 to 13 s after load (the example card was clicked at 9.7 s; I waited 3 s more). The example opens on the Tank section (screen 13) with a toast "Opened the example ... Undo brings the previous tank back; Save keeps your own copy." and verdict chip "Caution".
- Fastest path to a first fish in a blank tank: Blank tank -> Freshwater -> Animals -> Add on a neon tetra = 4 desktop clicks (the first tank is a 24 x 12 x 12 in, 15 gal bare tank); the phone needs one more step (Done, to reveal the tab bar).
- My walkthrough of the blank path to a "complete" tank (tank model, rock, substrate, 4 plants, 8 neons + a pleco, a filter) took about 19 interactions. Header cost went $77 (empty 15 gal default) -> $277 / $12 per mo / 35 min per wk.
- The phone's tab-bar buttons responded to touch events but not to synthesized mouse clicks in my emulation; I attribute that to my emulation, not to a product defect.

## Other observations to weigh (from the screens above)

- The Get started card is hidden by CSS while a panel is open; a new visitor starts with a panel open, so the five-step list appears only after Done and is collapsed.
- The default new tank is a 15 gal "Custom 24 x 12 x 12 in", not a purchasable model; "Pick a tank" is ticked only when the box differs from that default or a product is chosen.
- Section sub-titles are descriptive ("Model, water, stand, lid, background"), not outcome-based.
- "Ideas for Tank / Hardscape / Plants / Animals" buttons exist on every section (Discover engines); no screen explains them before clicking.
- Preferences and Constraints (not screenshotted; code in `src/shell/prefs.tsx`, `constraints.tsx`): the Constraints panel reads "Every limit lives here. Other sections read them; none keeps its own copy." with Set-up budget and Monthly limit fields (an "On" switch, no value), "No new equipment: spend nothing beyond what I have", Space "Not set", Experience "Count my experience: optional, Off", Your home "Not set".
- For a returning user: header Save / Open, Compare (A/B), Share link, Download; no tank library screen, tags or bulk actions were seen in the walk.

## What a good answer looks like

- Two short walkthroughs (beginner, expert), naming the exact screen numbers where each stalls, gets bored or is misled, tied to the vision ("anyone, every level"; experience is an optional gate; money matters; no care guides).
- Exactly five changes, ranked, each with: the screen(s) affected by number, the observed problem in one line, and what "fixed" looks like as something checkable on that screen (a visible text, a default, a click count, a before/after state), not a vague direction.
- Distinguishes defects (hidden confirmation dialog, header verdict moving to "Compatible" without a heater, library filter vs. row warning disagreement, clipped phone status strip) from design choices (settings-first Tank panel, collapsed Get started pill), and says which are cheapest to fix.
- Says what it cannot judge from stills (animation, real-GPU speed, a library of many saved tanks) instead of guessing.
