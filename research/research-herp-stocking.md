# Reptile and amphibian stocking research (2026-10-19)

Scope: the nine herps on the roster (dart frog, White's tree frog, fire-bellied toad, Chinese fire-bellied newt, African dwarf frog, axolotl, crested gecko x2 morphs, leopard gecko, ball python) plus the incoming anoles (lane AN: green, brown, knight).

**Evidence grades.** F = I fetched the page and read the numbers. S = taken from a search-result summary only, not opened. Most herp guidance is hobbyist or retailer opinion; vet and lab sources are flagged. Where sources disagree I give the range rather than pick a winner.

## Method: why floor space, and where it fails

Gallons hide the footprint (a 40 breeder is 4.5 sq ft, a 40 tall is about 3), so everything below is converted to sq ft of floor. Conversions I used: 10 gal = 20x10 in = 1.4 sq ft; 20 gal high = 24x12 = 2.0; 20 long = 30x12 = 2.5; 40 breeder = 36x18 = 4.5; 18x18 = 2.25; 24x18 = 3.0; 4x2 ft = 8.0. For aquatic species I convert gallon rules at the 20-long ratio (about 8 gal per sq ft), so "+10 gal" is roughly +1.25 sq ft.

Floor space is the right unit for ground dwellers (leopard gecko, ball python, fire-bellied toad, dart frog, axolotl, newt, ADF). It is the wrong unit alone for the **arboreal** ones (crested gecko, White's tree frog, anoles): their sources specify height first, so the tool needs a minimum height and should count usable volume or climbing surface, not floor alone.

## What each species lives with

Cross-species findings are the weakest part. The only sourced pairings are the aquatic ones; the rest rests on a general mixed-species warning plus basic biology.

| Animal | Good tankmates | Never |
|---|---|---|
| African dwarf frog | Small peaceful fish 3 in or under: rasboras, small tetras, corydoras, hatchetfish (S, several consumer sheets) | Bettas and fancy guppies (fin nipping), cichlids, anything aggressive or that fits in the frog's mouth; tiny shrimp and fry get eaten |
| Axolotl | Nothing (RVC vet: best alone; care sheets: fish nip gills, compete for food) | Fish, invertebrates that nip gills or fins, a much larger or smaller axolotl |
| Chinese fire-bellied newt | Cherry shrimp and snails (Josh's, F via search); no fish guidance found | Unverified for fish, so treat as "not recommended" |
| Fire-bellied toad | No source found | Treat as same species only |
| Dart frog | No source found | Treat as own species only; humidity needs clash with geckos and pythons |
| White's tree frog | Same-size White's | Smaller frogs (eaten); dry-ground species |
| Crested gecko, leopard gecko, ball python | Same species only, under the rules above | Each other and every amphibian: a leopard gecko needs a dry warm gradient, crested and the frogs need humidity; geckos and pythons eat small frogs |
| Jumping spider, tarantula (existing roster) | Alone (the tool already marks them solo) | Everything |

Supporting source for "mix rarely, never casually": amphibiancare.com "The Mixed Species Dilemma" and the Caudata species-mixing poll. They state the risks (space, predation, toxins, disease, different humidity) but do not name the pairings above, which are my inference.

## What this means for the tool

A rule record per species would carry: `social` (solo / pair / group), `maxGroup`, `sexRule` (no-male-pairs, one-male-only, any), `sizeMatch` (bool), `floorFirstSqFt`, `floorEachSqFt`, `minHeightIn`, `arboreal` (count volume), and `tankmates` (a species allow-list rather than a deny-list). The range columns above fit a low / recommended pair: use the larger value for the "recommended" line, the smaller for the "bare minimum" warning, and show the disagreement honestly (for example "vet says 10 gal, hobby standard 4.5 sq ft").
