# Substrate depth, anaerobic beds, and bottom-dweller substrate (lane SD1, 2026-11-12)

US units first, metric in brackets. 1 in = 2.54 cm.

**Evidence warning up front.** Nearly all of this field is hobby practice (care sheets, retailer guides, forums). I found **no controlled study** of aquarium planting depth, of Corydoras barbel erosion vs substrate, or of H2S in aquarium sand beds. The peer-reviewed sediment-chemistry work is on marine and lake sediments, and it supports mechanism, not aquarium thresholds. Every number below is therefore a hobby consensus range. Strength tags: **[consensus]** = several independent hobby sources agree; **[single]** = one source; **[anecdote]** = forum reports; **[science-mechanism]** = peer-reviewed, different system; **[unverified]** = a source cites it but I could not find the original.

Limits of this pass: I read search summaries for most pages and fetched four in full (Fuller, aquariumscience.org, Practical Fishkeeping, gensou.sg anaerobic guide). Many hits were low-authority retailer blogs (several on odd domains). Where only those exist, I say so. Nothing here is from Tropica or Walstad directly (searches did not surface them); that is the first thing to firm up.

## 1. Planting depth by plant group

| Group | Examples in our catalogue (`PlantType`) | Minimum | Recommended | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heavy root feeders | `sword`, `ozelot`, `crypt`, `balansae`, `sagittaria`, `lotus` | 2 in [5 cm] | 3-4 in [7.5-10 cm] | Swords want 4 in+ (large ones). Crypts and Sagittaria tolerate 2-3 in. |
| Stem plants | `stem`, `stemred`, `rotala`, `ludwigia`, `hornwort`, `anacharis`, `grass` (Val-type) | 1.5 in [4 cm] to anchor | 2-3 in [5-7.5 cm] | Mainly anchor; many feed from the water column. Hornwort/anacharis can float, no substrate. |
| Carpets | `carpet` (Monte Carlo, HC), `hairgrass` | 1.2 in [3 cm] | 1.5-2 in [4-5 cm] in front | Gensou: Monte Carlo 3-4 cm good, over 5 cm risks anaerobic pockets under the dense mat. |
| Epiphytes / rhizome | `fern`, `anubias`, `buce`, `moss` | none | none | Rhizome must stay **above** the substrate (burying it rots it). Tie/glue to hardscape. |
| Floaters | `redroot`, `salvinia`, `frogbit`, `lettuce` | none | none | |

Sources and strength:
- Swords and crypts ~3 in minimum, 4-5 in back, 2-3 in front: [consensus]. https://www.furrycritter.com/pages/articles/fish/substrate_for_plants.htm , https://planetcatfish.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=248682 , https://fishprofiles.com/forums/Planted_Aquaria/Gravel_Depth_for_New_Plants_an/13110 , https://enviroliteracy.org/animals/how-deep-should-planted-tank-substrate-be/
- Monte Carlo 3-4 cm in the foreground, >5 cm risks anaerobic pockets; iwagumi-style 5-8 cm rear sloping to 3 cm front: [single, retailer blog]. https://gensou.sg/monte-carlo-aquarium-plant-guide/
- Rhizome plants: keep the rhizome uncovered: [consensus, retailers]. https://aquasabi.com/aquascaping-wiki/aquatic-plants/planting-aquarium-plants , https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/super-glue-plants (Co-op is internally mixed; Splashy Fish says either works). Safe reading: exposed, anchored to hardscape.
- Disagreement: one forum voice argues Cryptocoryne and Echinodorus take most nutrients from the water column, so depth matters less than nutrient supply (root tabs). Most guides still push 3+ in with an inert substrate. [anecdote]. Depth is for anchoring and root volume; nutrition is a separate question (root tabs / soil).
- Species exceptions for the data: Vallisneria / Sagittaria spread by runners and root shallowly but widely (treat as 2 in min); tiger lotus is a bulb plant and wants 3-4 in; dwarf Sagittaria and small crypts (Wendtii) live fine at 2 in.

## 2. Too deep: anaerobic beds

What is established:
- **[science-mechanism]** Oxygen penetrates only millimeters into sediment, and finer, more organic sediment penetrates less: muddy beds ~2-3 mm, permeable sand ~4 mm at a smooth surface, deeper (13-40 mm) only with flow or surface relief. Mud is less porous so diffusion is limited and gradients are steep. https://hal.sorbonne-universite.fr/hal-01281737/document , https://int-res.com/abstracts/meps/v140/p227-237 , https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/153842/3/Oxic%20sediments.pdf . These are marine/lake studies; so "every bed has an anoxic layer a few mm down" is solid, but nothing says where it becomes harmful in a tank.
- Any bed deeper than a few mm is partly anoxic; that is normal and not itself a problem. Whether it produces harmful H2S depends on organic load, grain size, flow, and disturbance.

What the hobby says (depth thresholds disagree):
- "Over 4 in gives lots of anaerobic pockets" [anecdote, forum]; "over 5-6 in raises risk unless circulation is strong" [single]; "concern only above ~10 in if maintained" [anecdote]; gensou: over 6 cm [2.4 in] front or 8 cm [3.1 in] back is higher risk for fine **aquasoils** (they compact; coarse soils compact slower; a sand cap makes it worse) [single, unsourced]. https://gensou.sg/?p=40741
- A deep-sand-bed definition (reef hobby): fine sand, minimum 4-6 in; burrowing fauna overturn the top 2-3 in. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_sand_bed (marine, weak for freshwater).
- One source says year-long controlled experiments with 5 in sand beds found no H2S and no anaerobic denitrification. I could not find the original: [unverified]. aquariumscience.org states a deep sand bed "never becomes truly oxygen free" and describes a hypoxic zone (4-25 % saturation) where some bacteria produce toxins that stay locked in unless stirred up. [single, unsourced]. https://aquariumscience.org/index.php/14-2-substrates
- Signs: rotten-egg smell at water changes, black pockets against the glass, bubbles on disturbance, slowed root growth, corys avoiding the bottom.
- Mitigation: rooted plants over ~40 % of the footprint (single source), Malaysian trumpet snails (the standard hobby answer, [consensus] but no study I found), a substrate-level flow, coarser grain, avoiding fine sand pressed under soil, and venting gently (skewer, not stirring). 

How real is H2S poisoning? Real mechanism, rare in practice, and the documented cases are mostly poor maintenance plus disturbance of an old bed. Evidence is anecdotal; the "myth" side is also anecdotal. Honest stance: **low-probability, high-consequence, so advisory not welfare Caution.**

Proposed thresholds for checks (our judgment from the above, not a published standard):

| Grain | Advisory when bed deeper than | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| mud / aquasoil / fine sand | 3 in [7.5 cm] | compacts, least porous; gensou 2.4-3.1 in; Monte Carlo guide 5 cm |
| sand (0.5-2 mm) | 4 in [10 cm] | hobby 4 in forum threshold; deep-sand-bed norm is 4-6 in with fauna |
| gravel (2-5 mm) | 6 in [15 cm] | porous, hobby 5-6 in |
| pebble (>5 mm) | 8 in [20 cm] | very porous |
Cory-friendly shallow beds are a separate concern (section 4). Terrestrial grounds (soil, coco, bark, layered) are not checked.

## 3. Sloped beds
- Front 1-2 in [2.5-5 cm], back 3-4 in+ [7.5-10+ cm]; one guide gives 2-3 in front to 4-5 in back for tall tanks. [consensus] https://www.furrycritter.com/pages/articles/fish/substrate_for_plants.htm , https://planetcatfish.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=248682 . Iwagumi: 5-8 cm rear to 3 cm front (gensou Monte Carlo guide above).
- Our check should therefore use the depth **at the plant's position** (interpolated between `fl/fr/bl/br`, or terrain height), not the tank average.

## 4. Bottom dwellers

### Corydoras
- Sharp-edged substrate injures barbels, which can get infected and erode: stated by most care sources: Ian Fuller (long-time Corydoras authority) "any sharp edged sand or gravel can very quickly damage the delicate barbels"; recommends smooth fine gravel or coarse smooth sand **no deeper than 15 mm** [0.6 in] so fish reach the glass for buried food. https://www.corydorasworld.com/articles/ian-fuller/starting-with-corydoras . Practical Fishkeeping: fine silica sand, gravel can injure barbels; exceptions: *Scleromystax* (rocky/gravel habitat), pygmy species (*C. hastatus, C. pygmaeus*) and open-water swimmers don't strictly need fine substrate. https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/fishkeeping-answers/whats-the-best-substrate-for-corydoras/
- Grain: aquariumscience.org says gravel under 3/16 in [4.8 mm] for corys; pea gravel above ~0.3 in [7 mm] discouraged. [single] https://aquariumscience.org/index.php/14-2-substrates
- **Strength of evidence: weak-to-moderate.** No controlled study. A secondhand, unverifiable report (Ian Fuller, forum) says corys on broken glass did not show more erosion than on sand or bare bottom; high nitrate/infection correlated with erosion in the same thread. Other keepers report healthy corys on sharp gravel, and some call the gravel claim a myth; a breeder reported severe barbel loss in fry on fine sand and none on rounded gravel; one report of barbel loss on smooth pool-filter sand. So: smoothness matters in the consensus, water quality and infection are plausible co-causes. https://www.fishforums.net/threads/barbel-erosion.168736 , https://aquifarm.com/why-sharp-gravel-is-bad-for-corydoras , https://forums.thinkfish.co.uk/new-fishkeepers/is-pea-gravel-a-problem-for-corydoras-punctatus/msg6954
- **Our call:** corys PREFER sand / smooth fine gravel (soft advisory); sharp/rough substrate is a Caution because consensus is near-universal and the downside (infection) is real, even if the causal evidence is thin. Pygmy corys: lighter version (they do fine on gravel; still prefer fine).

### Kuhli loaches (*Pangio*)
- Soft sand is the near-universal recommendation; fine, smooth gravel acceptable; they bury partly or wholly. One guide says gravel damages barbels and bellies. No sourced depth; hobby rule is a sand layer 1-2 in deep so they can actually bury [reasoned, single]. [consensus on sand; no depth source]. https://www.aquariadise.com/caresheet-kuhli-loach/ , https://www.hepper.com/kuhli-loaches/ , https://gensou.sg/kuhli-loach-care-guide/
- **Our call:** NEEDS fine/soft (sand) substrate and at least ~1 in [2.5 cm] of it; coarse gravel, pebble, crushed coral, lava = Caution.

## 5. Where sources disagree and confidence
1. **Anaerobic threshold (4 in vs 6 in vs 10 in):** unresolved; depends on grain, flow, plants, snails. We use grain-dependent thresholds and word the advisory as "may". Confidence low; direction (finer and deeper = worse) high.
2. **H2S real vs myth:** mechanism certain, incidence unknown. Advisory only.
3. **Cory barbel/gravel causation:** consensus says sharp is bad; causal evidence is thin; water quality is a plausible confounder. Soft/Caution as above, never a hard block.
4. **Plant minimum depth:** consensus 3 in for swords/crypts; nutrition may matter more than depth. Confidence moderate for swords/crypts, low for carpets and stems.
5. **Corys depth ("no deeper than 15 mm")** (Fuller) conflicts with planted-tank advice of 2-3 in. Not modelled as a depth check, only mentioned in advisory copy; the sand-vs-gravel check matters more.
