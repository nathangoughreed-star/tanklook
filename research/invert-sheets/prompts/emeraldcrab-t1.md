MASTER GEOMETRY — MOST IMPORTANT
First establish one anatomically accurate three-dimensional specimen. All views must be derived from this specimen without changing its pose.
Carapace

* Adult carapace width: 1.5 inches.
* Carapace length: approximately 1.2 inches.
* Carapace width is measured perpendicular to the anterior-posterior body axis.
* Carapace is wider than it is long.
* Broad, rounded, compact carapace with a low, gently domed profile.
* Characteristic sculptured surface with shallow grooves, low bumps, and irregular lateral margins.
* Short, notched frontal margin between the eyes.

Appendages
The crab has exactly ten primary appendages:

* One pair of chelipeds (claw-bearing limbs).
* Four pairs of walking legs.

Each side of the body has exactly five appendages, attached in the following order from anterior to posterior:

1. Cheliped.
2. First walking leg.
3. Second walking leg.
4. Third walking leg.
5. Fourth walking leg.

All appendages originate from anatomically appropriate sockets beneath the lateral margins of the carapace.

* Two approximately equal, robust claws with blunt, hollowed, spoon-shaped fingertips characteristic of emerald crabs.
* Four pairs of moderately short, jointed walking legs covered with fine hairs.
* Walking legs extend laterally from the body, with the posterior legs angled slightly backward.
* All walking-leg tips terminate in pointed dactyls, not paddles.
* Two dark eyes on short stalks seated in sockets at the anterior corners of the carapace.
* Small, anatomically appropriate mouthparts beneath the frontal margin.

This is a compact marine crab, not a long-legged spider crab, swimming crab, lobster, or hermit crab.

* Both claws extend forward, slightly outward from the body.
