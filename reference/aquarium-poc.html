What to test
  1. Depth size: the three neons are at front / middle / back. Check that the on-screen ratio matches the simple formula in the readout.
  2. Fair comparison: turn on Compare (24×12×12 vs 24×12×24). The same mid-depth fish should be smaller in the deeper tank.
  3. Card limits: drag Yaw and the camera angle. Note where the angelfish starts to look like paper (the readout flags it).
  4. Overlap: the plant is in front of the angelfish. Move depths past each other and check that overlap is pixel-correct. Try each Edge mode.
  5. School: add a test school and judge whether cards read as fish en masse.

The surface is blended smoothly between the four corners (bilinear interpolation). Depths are physical, so Tank B gets the same thickness, not a proportional one. The plant sits on the surface; fish placed below it are hidden by it.

Compare

Tank B shows the same fish at the same relative positions (fractions of L/H/D). Both views use one camera rule and one field of view, so pixel sizes are directly comparable.

Camera (the viewer)

Distance is measured from the eye to the centre of the front glass and is the same for every tank. Zoom only crops; it doesn't change perspective.
