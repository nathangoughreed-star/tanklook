TankLook
Development priorities & roadmap
Build 560  •  Based on the supplied roster and previously reviewed model sheets
A visual sandbox for arranging realistically scaled living things and objects inside transparent 
enclosures—freshwater, marine, terrestrial, mixed, or unconventional.
Illustrative enclosure concepts: freshwater aquarium, arboreal vivarium, paludarium, arid terrarium, jumping-spider enclosure, and coral 
reef.
Executive recommendation
Prioritize capabilities that unlock entire enclosure categories before continuing broad species expansion. First, make 
dry terrestrial scenes convincing. Then add surface-aware placement and the initial reptile/invertebrate body plans. 
Continue marine and freshwater coverage selectively.
Product rule — One shared placement and visualization system, not a separate editor for each ecosystem.
Primary success metric — Can a user create a convincing representative scene quickly, at realistic scale, without 
workarounds?
All illustrations in this document are AI-generated conceptual references, not screenshots of TankLook or validated animal-model renders.

Architecture principle
A dry terrarium is a scene with no water; a paludarium is a scene with a partial water volume. They should use the 
same object, scale, placement, grouping, and rendering machinery. Do not introduce an ecosystem selector that 
arbitrarily disallows objects.

Pipeline prerequisite
Earlier model-sheet reviews found recurring structural issues in shrimp, snail shells, and species-defining fish fins. 
Those findings came from model v1 (build 217), not build 560; verify whether they remain before using the same 
generators to scale into reptiles and terrestrial invertebrates.
Fish can appear natural in an arbitrary suspended pose. Land animals usually cannot: a gecko must visibly cling to a 
surface, and a leopard gecko needs a convincing ground-contact stance. Pose and anchors are core visual 
requirements, not later polish.

Custom assets: the escape hatch
No finite official species library can cover everything. A transparent PNG import with a user-specified real-world size 
could instantly support unusual organisms or decorations. Full 3D imports can come later; both should honor the 
same coordinate and scaling system.
Modeling quality as shared infrastructure
Body plans — Use separate anatomical templates rather than adapting a generic fish-like shape.
Attachments — Parameterize fin bases, joints, legs, shell openings, eyes, mouths, and other structural landmarks.
Pose validation — Test front/side/top silhouettes and representative mounted poses before production release.
Lighting and materials — Evaluate transparent fins, hair, shells, and other materials in bright preview and tank 
lighting.

8  |  Four release milestones
Release 1 — Convincing dry enclosure
Dry substrates, terrestrial texture layers, wall materials, and a dozen starter plants/hardscape assets.
Acceptance test: A believable planted glass terrarium with no animal whatsoever.
Release 2 — Terrestrial animals
Surface anchoring, appropriate static poses, and five representative terrestrial species.
Acceptance test: Build crested gecko, leopard gecko, and jumping-spider setups without placement hacks.
Release 3 — Unified land and water
Partial-water regions, raised banks, emergent plants, and correct occlusion across the interface.
Acceptance test: Build a believable mixed land/water paludarium with branches, aquatic planting and frogs.
Release 4 — Diversity and freedom
Marine forms, selective freshwater species, efficient duplication/scattering, and initial custom import.
Acceptance test: Build reef, freshwater, terrestrial and mixed displays from the same editor.

Final recommendation
Make a fully convincing planted terrestrial enclosure the next major milestone, then add the first reptile and 
terrestrial-invertebrate body plans. Keep the composition editor ecosystem-neutral. Add aquatic species selectively 
where they introduce distinctly new silhouettes or habitats.
